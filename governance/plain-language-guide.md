# Plain-English Authoring Guide

The glossary keeps precise definitions, but precision should not require unnecessary complexity. A term may therefore include `simple_definition` as a short reader-facing explanation before the formal definition.

## Writing rules

1. Say what the thing is or does in the first sentence.
2. Prefer common words: use “use” instead of “utilize”, “show” instead of “demonstrate” where the meaning is unchanged.
3. Keep the simple definition to one sentence and normally 30 words or fewer.
4. Do not hide important limits. State the purpose or scope when leaving it out would mislead the reader.
5. Avoid defining a term only by repeating the term itself.
6. Keep protocol names, legal terms and established standards language when changing them would change the meaning.
7. Treat the formal `definition` as the precision layer and `simple_definition` as the comprehension layer. They must not contradict each other.

## Example

**Formal:** “A governed decision to accept another party, authority, status, credential, assurance result, or rule as valid for a defined purpose and scope.”

**Simple English:** “A decision to accept something or someone as valid for a particular purpose.”

## Migration approach

Plain-English coverage is incremental. Prioritize terms used at governance or implementation decision points, then high-traffic standards terms, then the remaining descriptive vocabulary. A missing simple definition is visible on the generated page rather than silently replaced with an unreliable automatic paraphrase.
